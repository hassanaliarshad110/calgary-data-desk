## Advisories (not blocking)

- **A1:** S3 says the wet-gas strike "did feed the oil speculation that had already erupted", so some speculation came before 14 May. The hook "started with a gas well" is still supported by S1 ("the province's first oil and gas boom was on") and S6 ("blew in, heralding an oil boom"). Keep the hook, but be aware of this nuance if a viewer challenges it.
- **A2:** S9's "a few kilometres southwest" understates the real distance. The video correctly uses only "southwest". Do not add a distance taken from S9.
- **A3:** "rich in a light liquid called naphtha" is slightly stronger than S7's "contained naphtha". It is supported by S4 (naphtha became the principal product). Optional safer wording: "that contained a light liquid called naphtha."
- **A4:** In the scene 1 hook illustration, "embers rising" suggests fire. The 1914 Dingman well did not burn (S2: wet gas "dowsing the workers"). It is labelled "illustration", but consider dropping the embers in scene 1. Keep fire for the 1924 Royalite scene and the flaring scene.
- **A5:** The year is spoken with a hyphen in L1-L3 ("nineteen-eleven") and without one in L10, L11 and L13 ("nineteen twenty-one"). This is cosmetic. Check that the voice reads both forms the same way.
- **A6:** In the teaser, T1's last line "But it wasn't the oil investors had hoped for" relies on T0 ("a gas well") for its meaning. It reads fine in sequence. Check that the vertical teaser also shows the follow pill, or that its post caption carries the follow line.
- **A7:** The "Plan a day trip into the mountains" call to action (Banff, Canmore, Kananaskis) is only loosely tied to Turner Valley. It is not a factual issue.
- **A8:** In the claims table, the C24 row sits between C20 and C21. This is cosmetic.
