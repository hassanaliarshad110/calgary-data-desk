Residential waste collection fees could go up nearly $6 by 2030: report

Scott Strasser
Updated Fri, October 2, 2026 at 6:06 p.m. UTC
4 min read
Add Yahoo as a preferred source to see more of our stories on Google.

While Calgarians' blue bin fees decreased $7 this year thanks to the adoption of a new provincewide recycling policy, the city is proposing annual rate hikes for its residential waste collection services over the next four years that would largely offset those cost savings.

An administrative report coming to city council's executive committee meeting Monday outlines proposed fee increases for Calgary's curbside waste collection programs, as well as higher tonnage fees at the city's landfills.

Collection fees for Calgary's black, blue and green cart programs are proposed to go up by $1.45 annually from 2027 to 2030, for a total increase of $5.80.

Currently, households spend $7.71 per month for black cart (garbage) collection, $2.17 for their blue cart (recyclables) and $10.63 for their green cart (organics), with those fees included on their monthly Enmax bill. Combined, the cart collection program costs participating residences $20.51 monthly.

If council supports the higher rates, that would climb to $26.31 per month by 2030.

Broken down, the monthly black cart fee would increase to $9.67 by 2030, while the blue cart would cost homes $2.81 and the green cart charge would be $13.83.

In April, council rejected a request from administration to consolidate the three collection fees into one monthly charge, arguing that lumping the fees together would reduce financial transparency.

At that same meeting, a representative from waste and recycling told council the city spends $30 million on its black cart service delivery; $43 million on blue cart collection and $49 million on green cart collection, per year. Thanks to $32 million the city now receives from the province through a new policy called Extended Producer Responsibility, the city's net annual budget for waste collection is $92 million.

Most of that is funded through the cart program fees — only $8.8 million, or 4.5 per cent, of the business unit's budget is supported by property taxes. The department aims to be fully supported by user fees by 2030, council heard.

In addition to higher charges for curbside pick-up, the city is also recommending a $5 increase next year to the basic sanitary rate for landfill drop-offs, which is currently $113 per tonne and has been for the last decade.

"After holding the basic sanitary disposal rate constant for the last 10 years, the proposed increase in 2027 responds to the increasing cost of disposal, particularly capital costs, and changes in economic conditions within the waste industry," the report states.

However, the city is recommending to keep the $3 garbage tag fee for excess black bin material, as well as the minimum charge at landfills, which is currently $25 per load, throughout the upcoming four-year cycle. The $25 rate mostly applies to residential customers who are bringing their excess waste to a landfill.

To respond to growth, inflation and overall higher costs related to waste and recycling services, the report says the city needs to buy more waste collection trucks, develop more facilities, build more landfill cells and hire more front-line staff.

Other higher costs relate to updated composting equipment and processing infrastructure and environmental control systems to ensure they protect public health, safety and the environment, while meeting regulatory requirements and supporting odour monitoring and reduction efforts in communities near waste facilities.

"Investment is needed to respond to growth and inflationary pressures, increasing visits and tonnage at waste management facilities, asset capacity and reliability risks, and evolving regulatory requirements," the report states.

While residential cart program fees were frozen throughout the 2019-22 budget cycle, a previous city council approved annual increases of $0.70 for the 2023-26 cycle, in addition to a subsequent $0.31 increase in 2025 and $0.45 in 2026 during mid-cycle budgetary adjustments.

However, the adoption last year of a new provincewide regulation, dubbed Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR), brought blue cart fees down more than $7 per month, from $9.34 to $2.17 per household. EPR also enabled a relinquishment of $500,000 in property tax support this year.

EPR is a new framework for recycling that transfers the cost of collecting, sorting and recycling certain materials from municipal governments to the manufacturers whose packaging produces the waste in the first place.

The second phase of the new policy went into effect Thursday and will allow Calgary households to deposit more material in their blue bins, including certain tubes and aerosols, as well as crinkly food and foam packaging.
