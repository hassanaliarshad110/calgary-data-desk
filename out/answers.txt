ANSWER:
The City's year-end operating surplus was $236.4 million in 2023, $276.3 million in 2024 and $259.3 million in 2025. The site holds only those three years, so I can't give the other two budgets from the data I can reach.

| Year | Operating surplus | Where it went (transfer to the Fiscal Stability Reserve, FSR) | Council draws from the FSR, approved in November of the same year |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | $236.4M | Transferred to the FSR in 2024 | $88M for one-time operating and capital investments, plus $100M for new capital from the 2023 surplus; $242M moved to the Major capital projects reserve |
| 2024 | $276.3M | Transferred to the FSR in 2025 | $61M for one-time operating and capital investments; $136M moved to the capital and lifecycle reserve |
| 2025 | $259.3M | Transferred to the FSR in 2026 | $317M for one-time operating and capital investments and heritage reserves; $15M to the Opportunity Calgary Investment Fund |

- **Where the surplus goes:** each year's operating surplus is transferred to the FSR in the next fiscal year. The FSR held $1,214M at the end of 2024 and $1,353M at the end of 2025, each including that year's surplus.
- **Is it used as relief later?** The reports show the FSR is a contingency fund. It covers emergencies, urgent or contingency capital, and unplanned revenue drops. Its investment income can fund one-time operating spending. The reports don't tie any year's surplus to a later spending decision or to property tax relief. Council approves draws from the reserve, and the draws shown are for one-time investments, not tax relief. The draws above total $188M (2023), $61M (2024) and $332M (2025), by my addition of the listed items.
- **Caution:** the 2023 figure is audited (2025 Annual Financial Report). The 2024 figure comes from the City's year-end update, which is not audited, because the 2024 Annual Financial Report does not include this reconciliation. The surplus is the City's "operating fund variance," not the consolidated annual surplus in the financial statements.
- **Not on the site:** the 2021 and 2022 surpluses. Those would be in the City's earlier Annual Financial Reports, which I can't read from here.

ANSWER:
The City's reserve is not held in a separate account. The Fiscal Stability Reserve (FSR) is an amount set aside in the City's books, and the cash behind it sits in one pooled City investment portfolio. The FSR held $1,353 million at the end of 2025. The report does not say which investments back any one reserve.

The portfolio was worth $6,759.2 million at 31 December 2025:

- Pooled fixed income: 37.6%
- Canadian fixed income: 23.4%
- Pooled equities: 17.3%
- Mortgages: 10.2%
- Infrastructure: 8.3%
- Real estate: 2.0%
- Foreign fixed income: 1.1%
- Global equities: 0% (held $138.7 million at the end of 2024)

Fixed income in total is about 62% of the portfolio. The City's report uses the term "fixed income" (bonds and similar), not "fixed deposits", so I can't confirm term deposits as a category. The average yield was 6.78% in 2025, up from 4.71% in 2024. About $617 million of the portfolio is managed in-house in money market instruments and bonds. The City could sell $3,641 million of its holdings within five business days.
