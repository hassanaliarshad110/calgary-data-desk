 FAQs for wastewater collection rates and bills
Wastewater collection rates 
 What services are included in the wastewater collection fees? 
Wastewater collection rates are designed to recover the costs for all sanitary and stormwater management services, flood mitigation, neighbourhood renewal and activities to protect the environment, such as biosolids disposal. (Biosolids are the by-product of the municipal wastewater treatment process.)</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-a85bdaa4d2" class="cmp-text ">
Wastewater collection rates are designed to recover the costs for all sanitary and stormwater management services, flood mitigation, neighbourhood renewal and activities to protect the environment, such as biosolids disposal. (Biosolids are the by-product of the municipal wastewater treatment process.) 
 What is the sanitary charge for? 
The sanitary charges are designed to recover the costs associated with planning, designing, building, operating and maintaining the pipes and facilities required for the collection and the transfer of wastewater from the places where we eat, work, play and visit to the Gold Bar Wastewater Treatment Plant.</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-0b466d17da" class="cmp-text ">
The sanitary charges are designed to recover the costs associated with planning, designing, building, operating and maintaining the pipes and facilities required for the collection and the transfer of wastewater from the places where we eat, work, play and visit to the Gold Bar Wastewater Treatment Plant. 
 What is the stormwater utility charge for? 
The stormwater charges are for the collection, quality enhancement and disposal of stormwater that runs off our streets and properties into the sewer system. It includes construction and maintenance of storm sewers, as well as other stormwater management/quality enhancement facilities.</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-567242872f" class="cmp-text ">
The stormwater charges are for the collection, quality enhancement and disposal of stormwater that runs off our streets and properties into the sewer system. It includes construction and maintenance of storm sewers, as well as other stormwater management/quality enhancement facilities. 
 How was the stormwater rate determined? 
The stormwater rate was set to ensure that the wastewater collection system can maintain services and infrastructure as well as meet its financial obligations in the future. This cost of providing stormwater services is distributed across all properties in Edmonton as it provides a community-wide benefit.</p>\r\n<p>Rate changes are reviewed and approved by our regulator, the City of Edmonton, and reflect the cost of providing service. Rates are set through a performance-based regulation process that balances reliable operations with the need to support growth across the city, while maintaining reasonable rates for customers.&nbsp;</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-ab11d5a4c4" class="cmp-text ">
The stormwater rate was set to ensure that the wastewater collection system can maintain services and infrastructure as well as meet its financial obligations in the future. This cost of providing stormwater services is distributed across all properties in Edmonton as it provides a community-wide benefit. 
Rate changes are reviewed and approved by our regulator, the City of Edmonton, and reflect the cost of providing service. Rates are set through a performance-based regulation process that balances reliable operations with the need to support growth across the city, while maintaining reasonable rates for customers.  
 Does everyone pay the same rate? 
All customers pay the same rate, while the daily charge will vary for everyone based on the size of your property and the amount of stormwater runoff from your property entering into the sewer system.&nbsp; The amount of runoff is determined based on the property zoning and varies with the permeability of a property’s surface (e.g., concrete vs. grass) and the amount of development (e.g., buildings vs. natural landscape).&nbsp;</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-de8cf5d20e" class="cmp-text ">
All customers pay the same rate, while the daily charge will vary for everyone based on the size of your property and the amount of stormwater runoff from your property entering into the sewer system.  The amount of runoff is determined based on the property zoning and varies with the permeability of a property’s surface (e.g., concrete vs. grass) and the amount of development (e.g., buildings vs. natural landscape).  
 Is GST charged to my wastewater collection charges? 
No, GST is not charged on wastewater collection charges.</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-622803a967" class="cmp-text ">
No, GST is not charged on wastewater collection charges. 
 Will the sanitary and stormwater rates increase in subsequent years? 
City Council, as regulator of EPCOR Water Services, approved an annual rate-setting formula under the performance based regulation structure for setting sanitary and stormwater rates for the 2025-2027 period. These annual rate changes are also reviewed and approved each year by the City.</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-a936ed2703" class="cmp-text ">
City Council, as regulator of EPCOR Water Services, approved an annual rate-setting formula under the performance based regulation structure for setting sanitary and stormwater rates for the 2025-2027 period. These annual rate changes are also reviewed and approved each year by the City. 
 Why are the sanitary and stormwater rate increases greater than the inflation rate? 
Increases to sanitary and stormwater rates are required to cover the ongoing costs to maintain and upgrade the stormwater system to ensure reliable stormwater services are provided to customers.</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-a8bdd9a911" class="cmp-text ">
Increases to sanitary and stormwater rates are required to cover the ongoing costs to maintain and upgrade the stormwater system to ensure reliable stormwater services are provided to customers. 
Sanitary utility bill 
 How are the sanitary charges calculated? 
The sanitary charges include a fixed rate that varies based on the size of water meter and a variable rate per cubic meter of water consumed. The rates are applied consistently to all residential and non-residential customers.</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-eddc5891ae" class="cmp-text ">
The sanitary charges include a fixed rate that varies based on the size of water meter and a variable rate per cubic meter of water consumed. The rates are applied consistently to all residential and non-residential customers. 
 What is the monthly water consumption for a typical residential household? 
A typical residential Edmonton household used 14.3 cubic meters (14,300 litres) of water per month in 2025.</p>\r\n<p>Household water usage can vary depending on household size, appliance/water fixture efficiency, and outdoor water use. A water leak can also waste significant amounts of water.</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-ce6e9cc621" class="cmp-text ">
A typical residential Edmonton household used 14.3 cubic meters (14,300 litres) of water per month in 2025. 
Household water usage can vary depending on household size, appliance/water fixture efficiency, and outdoor water use. A water leak can also waste significant amounts of water. 
 My toilet was leaking in our house for over a period of a few months. Will you give me a credit on my wastewater collection charges since I didn't use the water? 
Since the water still entered the sewer system, and therefore needed to be collected and conveyed to the wastewater treatment plant, no credits will be given.</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-246250233a" class="cmp-text ">
Since the water still entered the sewer system, and therefore needed to be collected and conveyed to the wastewater treatment plant, no credits will be given. 
 How is meter size determined for new commercial sites that are being constructed? 
This is done by EPCOR Water Services (EWS). EWS has a commercial meter inspector who is responsible for determining meter sizes based on site inspections and water demand analysis. A meter size that is too small for the amount of water required will result in insufficient water pressure to meet customer needs.</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-118fe93f18" class="cmp-text ">
This is done by EPCOR Water Services (EWS). EWS has a commercial meter inspector who is responsible for determining meter sizes based on site inspections and water demand analysis. A meter size that is too small for the amount of water required will result in insufficient water pressure to meet customer needs. 
 What is the process for customers requesting to downgrade their water meter size? 
The size of a water meter is dependent on the size of the water lines at the location. Customers may have reduced water flow if they change to a smaller meter. Stormwater utility bill 
What is stormwater? 
Stormwater refers to rainwater, snowmelt or excess water from lawn watering that flows off streets and lots. The storm sewer system collects stormwater and disposes it into stormwater ponds and creeks, and eventually, the North Saskatchewan River.</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-2e1d3bffbf" class="cmp-text ">
Stormwater refers to rainwater, snowmelt or excess water from lawn watering that flows off streets and lots. The storm sewer system collects stormwater and disposes it into stormwater ponds and creeks, and eventually, the North Saskatchewan River. 
About your monthly stormwater charges 
Stormwater utility charges cover the cost to build, operate, and maintain the stormwater infrastructure, which collects accumulated rainwater and snowmelt to prevent flooding and property damage. This cost is distributed across all properties in Edmonton as it provides a community-wide benefit. All customers pay the same rate, while the monthly charge will vary for everyone based on the size of your property, the amount of development (e.g., erected buildings vs. natural landscape) and how permeable your property's surface is (e.g., concrete vs. grass).&nbsp;</p>\r\n<p>The monthly stormwater utility charge was approved by City Council on July 2, 2002, and took effect January 1, 2003. Stormwater is considered a utility because it provides a base service and benefit to customers. The charge is for the base service, just like there is a basic or flat service fee each month for the use of the sanitary sewer. In both Canada and the U.S., other municipalities have implemented stormwater utility charges.</p>\r\n<p><b>Types of activities covered by the Stormwater Utility Charge</b></p>\r\n<ul>\r\n<li><b>Build</b></li>\r\n<li><b>Operate</b></li>\r\n<li><b>Maintain</b></li>\r\n</ul>\r\n"}}" id="text-2b6f1de031" class="cmp-text ">

 How is the Stormwater charge calculated?  
In accordance with the City of Edmonton EPCOR Wastewater Services Bylaw 20865 , the daily stormwater charge is calculated using the following formula: A x I x R x Rate , where:  
 A: Area  - The area of the property in square metres (m 2 ), and the proportion of the building lot area attributable to each unit for multiple units sharing a single building of property.  
 I: Development Intensity Factor -   The measure of the portion of lot being used for its intended development. The development intensity factor is 1.0 as default, except for commercial or multi-family residential properties where owners demonstrate they contribute significantly less stormwater runoff per property area to the City's sewer system during rainfalls than other similarly zoned properties by making an application for a reduction in the intensity development factor pursuant to the bylaw. 
 R: Runoff coefficient  - represents the portion of rainfall that becomes runoff and enters the stormwater system. The runoff coefficient relates to the portion of permeable and impermeable areas within your property and is determined based on the zoning.  
 Rate: The stormwater utility rate - the daily rate as of April 1, 2026 is $0.00327. 
 Don't we already pay for stormwater through property taxes? 
Not since the stormwater utility charge was approved by City Council on July 2, 2002 and took effect January 1, 2003. The monthly stormwater utility charge on your EPCOR bill replaces paying for the system through property taxes.</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-f3df3452bd" class="cmp-text ">
Not since the stormwater utility charge was approved by City Council on July 2, 2002 and took effect January 1, 2003. The monthly stormwater utility charge on your EPCOR bill replaces paying for the system through property taxes.

 Why is my neighbour paying less than me? 
Your neighbour's lot may be smaller.&nbsp;The charge is not based on the value of a property but the size of the lot, the land zoning type of the property and development intensity.</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-c2813b1843" class="cmp-text ">

 I have rain barrels that catch all the rain from my roof. Can I get a rebate or credit for conserving and capturing stormwater? 
There is no credit program for residential customers for capturing and containing stormwater in rain barrels or through absorbent landscaping. However, you may want to check out the&nbsp;<a href=\"/content/epcor/home/ca/en/ab/edmonton/conservation/incentives/rainwise-stormwater-rebate-program.html\" style=\"background-color: rgb(255, 255, 255);\">RainWise Rebate Program</a>&nbsp;to see if any of your future landscaping projects qualify for those rebates.</p>\r\n"}}" id="text-dbbaf0fa32" class="cmp-text ">
There is no credit program for residential customers for capturing and containing stormwater in rain barrels or through absorbent landscaping. However, you may want to check out the  RainWise Rebate Program  to see if any of your future landscaping projects qualify for those rebates. 
 Is there a rebate or credit program for residential customers? 
Residential properties are considered to fully reflect their intended land use, so there is currently no credit program in place.</p>\r\n<p>EPCOR offers stormwater rebates through the <a href=\"/content/epcor/home/ca/en/ab/edmonton/conservation/incentives/rainwise-stormwater-rebate-program.html\" target=\"_self\">RainWise Rebate Program</a>. We recommend you look at that program to see if any future landscaping projects qualify for those rebates (e.g. rain barrels, rain garden, etc.).&nbsp;<b></b></p>\r\n"}}" id="text-de7947a737" class="cmp-text ">
