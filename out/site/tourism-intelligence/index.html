The visitor economy, 2025

10.5 million visitors spent $3.3 billion in Calgary Estimate. Hotel occupancy was 67.5% and short-term rental occupancy 62% Estimate; 84,000 Calgarians work in tourism and hospitality, counting seasonal and part-time jobs.

The gateway: what lies within a few hours

Alberta has six UNESCO World Heritage Sites, more than any other province; five are within about four and a half hours of Calgary: the Canadian Rocky Mountain Parks (Banff, Yoho, Kootenay, Jasper), Head-Smashed-In Buffalo Jump, Dinosaur Provincial Park, Waterton Glacier International Peace Park and Writing-on-Stone/Áísínai'pi. The sixth, Wood Buffalo, is in the far north. Banff National Park had 4,500,000 visitors in 2025-26 Count; Kananaskis Country about 4,900,000 visits in 2024 Estimate. Banff's commercial space is capped by federal law (350,000 sq ft of new commercial floor area set in 1998, all allocated by 2013); a 175-room hotel at 600 Banff Avenue may be the last new hotel in Banff. With Banff's visitors at a record 4.5 million, new beds for Rockies visitors have to come from Canmore (itself limited) or Calgary.

Infrastructure, incentives and investment

  • Stopover pilot. A pilot: travellers on WestJet's London Heathrow and Edinburgh routes add a Calgary stay of up to seven days at no extra airfare, with 19 hotel offers and the Calgary Attractions Pass; more routes and markets planned. Tourism Calgary and WestJet launch Calgary Stopover Program.
  • Levies. Alberta tourism levy 6%; Up to 6% of room revenue, charged by about 60 Calgary Hotel Association hotels; voluntary for guests today. The Traveller Protection and Destination Development Act takes full effect 1 January 2027: the full price including the destination marketing fee shown at booking, the fee held by a third-party trustee, one approved fee and one destination marketing organization per community. Hotel and lodging tax in Calgary 2026, RoomMaster (secondary source; check the Act and Alberta's levy page).
  • Rail. Alberta's passenger rail master plan puts airport links for Calgary and Edmonton (LRT extensions) first, with $15 million over three years for planning; Calgary to Banff and Edmonton to Calgary high-speed rail are targets within about 30 years; full build-out at least $60 billion. A private proposal (Liricon Capital) for Calgary to Banff beside the CPKC line was not preferred in the plan. Alberta advances plans for passenger rail lines throughout the province, Calgary Journal, 8 June 2026.
  • Indigenous tourism. Tourism Calgary and Indigenous Tourism Alberta signed an MOU on 23 January 2025: $1 million over four years (with the Calgary Hotel Association) to develop and promote Indigenous experiences, and Tourism Calgary pays membership dues for Treaty 7 and Metis District businesses. One in three international visitors seek an authentic Indigenous experience; two in three meetings, conventions and major events coming to Calgary want one. Indigenous Tourism Alberta and Tourism Calgary sign MOU. Glenbow reopening.
  • Kananaskis. The province plans more campsites, trails and parking, a Highway 742 upgrade, and is exploring the All-Season Resorts Act for Kananaskis (its estimate: $2 billion a year in visitor spending); conservationists warn of crowding and wildlife impacts.
  • The Stampede. $721 million economic impact across Alberta in 2025 Estimate. Calgary Stampede economic impact report shows 33 per cent jump in 6 years, LiveWire Calgary, 5 February 2026.

Calgary and Dubai: two models

Dubai is the reference most often cited for tourism growth. The two are not like for like: Dubai is a global air hub with year-round sun, visa policy set nationally and a state-led plan; Calgary is a mid-sized gateway to mountain parks, with federal visa rules and a seasonal peak. The comparison shows the levers, not a target.

What other destinations did

  • Iceland (Icelandair stopover). A free stopover of up to seven days on transatlantic tickets turned a connection point into a destination; Iceland's visitors roughly tripled from 2010 to 2016, with Icelandair's marketing a major driver. Later, crowding and pressure on housing became issues. For Calgary: The closest model to the Calgary Stopover pilot (WestJet as the hub airline, YYC as the connection point). Icelandair layover travel, Fortune, 23 October 2016.
  • Singapore (Free Singapore Tour). Since 1987, transit passengers with 5.5 to 24 hours between flights get a free 2.5-hour city tour (Changi Airport, Singapore Airlines and the tourism board); over 80,000 took it in 2019, when transit passengers were about 30% of Changi's traffic. For Calgary: A short free tour (downtown, the river, the Peace Bridge, or a mountain-view drive) for long connections at YYC. Free Singapore Tour returns, Singapore Tourism Board, April 2023.
  • Dubai. 19.59 million international overnight visitors in 2025, a third record year, hotel occupancy 80.7% across 154,000 rooms; credited to a city economic plan (D33), year-round festivals (Shopping Festival, Summer Surprises, Fitness Challenge), visa policy, and marketing partnerships with hotel and payment brands. For Calgary: Signature festivals in the slow months, and partnerships with airlines, hotels and card brands. Dubai tourism hits record 19.59m visitors in 2025, Khaleej Times, 9 February 2026.
  • Airline stopovers (Qatar, Emirates, Etihad, Turkish, TAP, Copa). Hub airlines turn connections into stays: TAP free stopovers up to 10 days; Qatar discounted hotel packages for 12 to 96 hours; Emirates free hotel for 8 to 26 hour connections; Etihad and Turkish free hotel nights; Copa free 24-hour to 7-day stopovers. For Calgary: Calgary's stopover could add hotel or attraction value, not only free airfare, and extend beyond UK routes. Guide to airline stopover programs, The Points Guy.
  • New York City. 65 million visitors in 2025 (52.4 million domestic, 12.5 million international, down 3.2%), $84.7 billion economic impact, 397,000 jobs, hotel occupancy 84.2%; 1,515 meetings and events booked; the World Cup 2026 expected to bring 1.2 million visitors. For Calgary: Conventions and major events as a steady base; a city of Calgary's size can win events in their category. NYC Tourism + Conventions annual report, NYC Tourism + Conventions, 24 March 2026.
  • Denver (another gateway to the Rockies). 37.6 million visitors and $10.5 billion in 2025; 42% of overnight visitors arrive by air; a measured ad campaign returned $219 in visitor spending per $1; record convention centre attendance; overnight visits spread evenly across the year (22 to 28% a quarter). For Calgary: Market the city as a destination in its own right, not only a road to the mountains; even seasonality is possible for a Rockies gateway. Denver tourism sets records in 2025 with 37.6M visitors, Denver7, 21 May 2026.
