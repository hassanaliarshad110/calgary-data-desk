EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The Public Service recommends approval of 2026 and 2027 water and sewer volume rates.
There are no recommended changes to the Council approved 2026 to 2027 daily basic rates.
The water and sewer rate changes recommended in this report are influenced by:
• Addition of the NEWPCC Upgrade: Nutrient Removal Facilities (NR) project. The NR
project has a Class 3 cost estimate of $1.491 billion with an approved budget of $29
million. The sewer rate model assumes 2/3 funding from other levels of government. The
City’s 1/3 share is primarily funded by long term debt.
• Revised funding (government and debt) applicable to NEWPCC Upgrade: Biosolids
Facilities (Biosolids) project.
• Updated adjustments to the water and sewer fund operating budgets.
• Freezing the dividend paid by the Water and Sewer Utility into the General Revenue
Fund at the levels approved in the 2024 to 2027 Multi-Year Budget.
The revised rates include most recent updates to the operating budget, to the date of this report.
Given the funding assumptions, the proposed water and sewer volume rates are:

REASON FOR THE REPORT
Increase in water and sewer volume rates beyond inflation or changes to the dividend require
Council approval.
IMPLICATIONS OF THE RECOMMENDATIONS
The Public Service has evaluated the impact of costs related to NR capital project. The
proposed sewer volume rate is based on approximately $468 million in additional long-term
debt issuance over 50 years, plus an estimated financing costs of $79 million, and receiving
provincial and federal government funding estimated at approximately $994 million or
approximately two-thirds of the capital cost.
The Public Service has also incorporated an additional $334 million in funding from other levels
of government for the Biosolids capital project. This is offset with a decrease in debt
requirements of $334 million for this project.
The City of Winnipeg understands that some customers may have difficulties paying their utility
bill and offers the following options to assist:
• Payment arrangements are considered if more time is needed to pay utility bills.
• The Water Leak Credit Policy provides financial help to single-family residential and non-
profit customers who receive a high utility bill for water and sewer services, as a result of
water leak within their house or building.
• The H2O Help to Others program provides support to low-income individuals and
families who have difficulty paying their City of Winnipeg utility bill.
HISTORY/DISCUSSION
The Public Service employs industry best practices to establish water and sewer rates. The
rates are designed to fund all costs of operations and capital projects related to water and sewer
quality, city growth and regulatory requirements.
On September 29, 2023, Council approved an increase of $482 million, from $553 million to
$1.035 billion for the Biosolids capital project.
On January 25, 2024, Council approved the 2024-2027 water and sewer rates. At that time, the
Public Service identified two items would require a report back to Council for rate adjustments:
• If the City was unable to secure an increase in funding for the Biosolids project
• When the Class 3 cost estimate is complete for the NR project

1. MAJOR COMPONENTS IMPACTING 2026 AND 2027 SEWER RATES
1.1 Capital Spending and Associated Funding
The water and sewer financial plans are based on cash flows associated with The Public
Service’s asset management strategy and capital budget (Appendix A). Capital improvement
programs are funded through a combination of debt, reserves, equity and other levels of
government.
The NEWPCC Upgrade, the City’s largest and most complex capital project, includes three
major project components, totaling over $3 billion. Funding for Headworks Facilities has been
confirmed. Funding for NR and Biosolids projects remain in active discussion with both levels of
government, and projected funding contributions are included in the 2026 sewer rate model.
The City also anticipates securing a 50-year debt issuance estimated at $468 million for the NR
project, which has an existing authorized budget of $29 million. This capital project was referred
to the 2026 budget process.
Proposed changes to the funding formula in the rate model are summarized in Table 1 below
and include the following estimates:
• Biosolids: Additional $334 million government funding offset by a reduction of $334 million of
debt.
• NR: Additional $994 million from other levels of government, $29 million of approved City
funding plus $468 million of additional City debt . Additional associated debt financing
charges estimated at $79 million will be funded from retained earnings.

1.2 Long-Term Debt Issues
The 2026 sewer volume rate model incorporates additional debt funding for the NR project.
Total additional expected City debt funding is estimated at $468 million. Beginning in 2026, the
debt service costs are incorporated into the sewer rate model over a 50-year issue period.
With the inclusion of additional funding from other levels of government for the Biosolids
project, City debt requirements for this project are expected to decrease by approximately $334
million.
1.3 Financial Stability
The working capital target provides medium-term stability, adequate liquidity to sustain
operations and the ability to absorb losses in any given year when consumption falls short of
anticipated levels and/or unforeseen emergency funds are required.
Industry best practices recommend utilities maintain a minimum working capital target. The
Water and Waste Department follows best practices and strives for a combined water and
sewer working capital target of approximately eight percent or between 30 to 60 days cash and
investments on hand.
Cost increases in Provincial licence driven capital, capital transfers and retained earnings
funded capital projects have resulted in the necessity to increase funding, specifically in the
near term. The Public Service has lowered the projected working capital target in the peak
construction periods of the Biosolids and NR projects to mitigate the impact to the Utility
customer. The Public Service will continue to monitor working capital and adjust future
expenditures and rates to balance stability of the utility and affordability for customers.

3. RISKS
Primary risks associated with the 10-year financial projections in the 2026-2035 water and
sewer rate model include:
3.1 Combined Sewer Overflow (CSO) Mitigation
The CSO Master Plan’s estimated capital cost to meet the 85% capture control target is $1.15
billion in 2019 dollars. This is a class 5 planning level estimate which has an expected accuracy
range of -50 percent to +100 percent. Factoring in inflation and escalation over a multi-decade
program provides a wide range of potential total program capital costs. The Public Service
assumes that the current $30 million annual rate of program funding, which was increased by
50% annually between 2024-2027 and will also increase by $1 million annually beginning in
2029, will be sufficient to complete the program by 2045.
3.2 Debt
The current rate model assumes secure funding for the NR project, including agreements with
the provincial and federal governments, and acquiring 50-year debt issue for an estimated $468
million. Additional associated debt financing charges estimated at $79 million, will be funded
from retained earnings, and subsequently capitalized once the NR project has reached
substantial completion.
Should additional government funding for the NR and/or Biosolids projects not materialize or be
delayed, and/or the City not be successful in securing the additional debt these costs would
need to be funded by sewer volume rates alone.
3.3 New Legislation
8

=== page
Environmental and public health concerns are driving more stringent standards in the water and
sewage industry. The Environment Act Licences for the three sewage treatment plants have
required large increases in capital and operating costs. Further introduction of new, or more
stringent, legislation could cause an increase in rates.
3.4 Phasing out the Dividend
There are no options to decrease the dividend in the recommended 2026 and 2027 sewer
volume rates. Options to gradually phase out the dividend will be part of a separate report
expected for the next multi-year budget beginning 2028. Any changes to the dividend are
expected to impact the General Revenue Fund.
3.5 Cost Escalation
Cost escalation caused by inflation, supply chain issues, tariffs and market volatility may result
in further increases in expenditures in multiple areas (commodities, materials, chemicals, etc.).
4. COMPETITIVENESS
Winnipeg’s combined water and sewer rate remains competitive with other Canadian cities of
similar size. In 2025, Winnipeg ranks third out of five cities for affordability for residential
customers and second out of five cities for commercial customers. (Appendix C)
