Are new homes keeping up?

Homes built since 2021 are about as near as older homes to playgrounds (68% within 400 m, against 72%) and parks (97% within 400 m, against 99%), but farther from transit stops (70% within 400 m, against 97%), community centres (18% within 800 m, against 41%), indoor recreation centres (30% within 2 km, against 52%) and libraries (33% within 2 km, against 53%).

Near, but crowded (playgrounds): 43 communities have most homes within 400 m of a playground, yet what is within reach is shared by more children than Calgary's average (score under 0.80; 32 of them under every assumption). Examples: Cityscape 0.28, Falconridge 0.38, Skyview Ranch 0.39, Redstone 0.39, Carrington 0.40.
Near, but crowded (parks): 108 communities have most homes within 400 m of a park, yet what is within reach is shared by more residents than Calgary's average (score under 0.80; 93 of them under every assumption). Examples: Lower Mount Royal 0.05, Beltline 0.07, Bankview 0.15, Tuxedo Park 0.16, Downtown Commercial Core 0.17. The typical community scores 0.76 for parks: Calgary's figure is an average over every resident and is lifted by the communities next to a few very large parks, so 110 of 215 communities fall under 0.80.

Crowding counts everyone who can reach a playground or park, from any community: children from the next community who use the same playground share it too. "Near, but crowded" uses the score below (the community's crowding against Calgary's).

How much the estimate moves: Calgary's playground figure (within 400 m) under each way of counting residents: City's total, actual growth 4.08 (in use) · City's total, one rate 4.08 · growth model 3.99 · 2021 Census 4.62 playgrounds per 1,000 children.

Crowding spreads the estimated children (0 to 14) and residents of each community evenly over its homes (the 2021 Census plus modelled growth, not counted), counts each playground as one whatever its size, and will be firmed up with the 2026 Census age data in 2027. It is shown beside Calgary's figure only: no tier, no combined score.

What these figures do not measure
Straight-line distance, not walking distance: walking routes are longer (curved streets, rivers, rail lines, major roads), so every share is the most generous reading.
The City's inventory only: school, developer, condominium, community association and private amenities are not counted.
A transit stop is not a service level: how often buses come is not measured.
The size and condition of amenities are not measured: each playground counts as one, whatever its size.
Park crowding counts each park's whole area, natural areas and regional parks included, so homes near a very large park (Nose Hill, Fish Creek) show very high hectares per 1,000 residents.
Rental units are partly estimated: purpose-built rental buildings count as their units, from building permits or, where no permit matches, estimated from assessed value.
Children and residents are spread evenly over the homes of each community, from the population model's estimates (the 2021 Census plus modelled growth), not counted. The crowding figures will be firmed up with the 2026 Census age data in 2027.
New communities on the city's edge are naturally farther from shared facilities (recreation centres, libraries), some planned for later phases.
Community centre data needs checking: several large communities show no community centre within 800 m, which may mean community association halls are missing from the City's list.
