# The Georgia town behind Calgary's Field of Crosses
Date/slot: 2026-11-11 AM · Type: quick-insight (fun fact) · Skill: calgary-data-explainer · Category: Fun fact
Calendar topic: "Field of Crosses: the Memorial Drive tribute that appears every November" (kept; posted on Remembrance Day). Angle: the idea came from roadside crosses in Menlo, Georgia. Not in log.json or on the covered list.

## 1. Five potential topics / angles (researched this run)
1. Origin: Calgary businessman Murray McCann was moved by roadside crosses honouring the fallen in Menlo, Georgia, and brought the idea to the Calgary Poppy Fund (Calgary Foundation CKC profile; Veterans Affairs Canada).
2. Growth: started in 2009 with a few hundred crosses in a park on Memorial Drive (VAC says 500; the board chair quoted by CP24 says 300); more than 3,700 in 2026 (Global News, Nov 4, 2026; CTV/CP24, Nov 1, 2026).
3. Each cross carries the name, age, rank, unit and death date of a fallen southern Albertan (CKC).
4. "Night of Lights": lanterns in front of every cross on the evening of Nov. 10, burning until sunrise on Remembrance Day (Global News).
5. From private memorial to public charity: for ten years funded by a private foundation, then gifted to the public as a registered charity (CKC).

## 2. Recommendation
Angle 1 + 2 as one mini-story: a surprising origin (a small town in Georgia) with a beginning, a turn and an ending (a few hundred → 3,700+), told on Remembrance Day itself. One idea, no statistics study, numbers only where the fact is a number.

## Methodology and limitations
- Sources differ on the cross count (3,600 on the CKC profile; "over 3,000" on the VAC page; 3,700 in both 2026 news reports). The video uses the 2026 news figure "more than 3,700" for this year. The 2009 starting count differs (VAC: 500; Field of Crosses board chair Sean Libin quoted by CP24: "grown from 300 crosses to 3,700"), so the video says "a few hundred". Audit round 1 also corrected scene 3 from "Each one honours a fallen southern Albertan" (a separate 120-cross memorial honours Canadians from across the country, per CKC) to Global News's collective wording.
- The official fieldofcrosses.com site did not load in this run and is not cited.
- No numbers computed.
