# Calgary cloned a war memorial: the Memorial Drive poplars
Date/slot: 2026-11-12 PM · Type: quick-insight (fun fact) · Skill: calgary-data-explainer (lead identity Data Explainer, directed with calgary-motion-director) · Category: Fun fact
Calendar topic: "Memorial Drive's trees were planted for Calgary's First World War soldiers" (kept, sharpened to the surprising part: the City grew clones of the memorial poplars from cuttings). Verified this run on the City of Calgary's Memorial Drive trees page and Veterans Affairs Canada. Not in log.json or on the covered list: the Nov 11 AM fun fact was the Field of Crosses (a different tribute on Memorial Drive, with no mention of the trees), and the Nov 11 flagship covered Mewata Armoury, Currie Barracks and The Military Museums.
Format: 1080×1920 vertical, 30 fps, target 20-25 s, Kokoro `af_heart`, free motion-engine render.

## 1. Five potential topics / angles (researched this run)
1. The clones: in 2001 the City took live cuttings from the remaining memorial trees and began growing cloned memorial trees at a tree farm in Grand Forks, B.C. (City of Calgary, Memorial Drive trees).
2. The purpose: after the First World War, groups and individuals set out to plant a tree for each fallen soldier of the Great War, a living memorial for families with no grave to visit (City of Calgary).
3. The first tree: Mayor Adams planted the first tree on Sunnyside Boulevard, now Memorial Drive, in May 1922 (City of Calgary; Veterans Affairs Canada plaque text).
4. Only one male: "All the poplar trees along Memorial Drive are female, but one." (City of Calgary). Strong, but no detail on why; kept as a possible future fact.
5. A carved trunk: in 2003 a memorial poplar trunk was carved into the likeness of Sergeant George Ross Thompson (City of Calgary). Not used.
Numbers left out on purpose: the City page gives a grand total of 3,278 trees planted 1922-1928, while Veterans Affairs Canada says about 1,700 by 1927 and gives May 10 (City: May 11). Because the sources differ, the video gives no tree count and no day, only the year 1922, on which both agree.

## 2. Recommendation
Angles 3 + 2 + 1 as one mini-story: a twist hook ("Calgary cloned a war memorial") → when and why the trees were planted → the cuttings and clones → why (so the memorial poplars can be replaced by their own clones). It is light, surprising and shareable, and fits the week after Remembrance Day without repeating the Field of Crosses or the military-sites flagship.

## Methodology and limitations
- All facts come from the City of Calgary's own Memorial Drive trees page, with the first-tree and year facts also on the Veterans Affairs Canada page (plaque text). Nothing computed.
- The day of the first planting (May 10 vs May 11) and the number of trees (3,278 by 1928 vs about 1,700 by 1927) differ between the sources, so neither is used.
- The City page says the cloned trees were grown at a tree farm in Grand Forks, B.C.; the video says "in B.C." and shows "Grand Forks, B.C." on a schematic card.
- "Calgary cloned a war memorial" is a plain-language summary (interpretation) of claims 5 and 6: the memorial is the trees, and the City grew clones of them.
