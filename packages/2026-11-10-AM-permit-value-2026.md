## 2. Recommendation
Topics 1 + 2 as one insight: the total is up, but the growth came entirely from non-residential applications while residential fell. From January to September 2026, building permit applications in the City of Calgary listed $6.40 billion in estimated construction value, up about 10% from $5.80 billion in the same months of 2025. Residential applications fell from $4.38 billion to $3.83 billion (down $549 million, or 12.5%). Non-residential applications, such as commercial and industrial, rose from $1.40 billion to $2.52 billion (up $1.12 billion, or about 80%). These are estimated values on permit applications, not finished construction. ## Methodology and limitations
- Boundary: City of Calgary (permits the City receives), not the CMA.
- Measure: "Estimated Value" on building permit applications received (the statement is an application statement), not issued permits, starts or completions. Applications can be withdrawn or change.
- Period: year to date, January 1 to September 30, 2026, against the same period of 2025 as printed in the same statement (last-year figures as currently held in the City's system).
- Non-residential = the statement's NON-RESIDENTIAL TOTAL (industrial, commercial, general, institutional, government, retaining walls). A small "Unspecified" group is excluded from both sides of the residential/non-residential comparison.
- Nominal dollars, not adjusted for construction-cost inflation. The video does not explain why non-residential rose (no cause is claimed); "Commercial New" applications ($1.08B vs $0.35B) were the largest single increase.
