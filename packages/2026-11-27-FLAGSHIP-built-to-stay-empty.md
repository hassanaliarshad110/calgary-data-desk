## 3. Why it matters
The 2013 flood was the largest in Calgary since 1897 and the biggest shock to the city in a generation. Most Calgarians know the flood; few know what has been built since, that one of the biggest pieces is a reservoir that is dry almost all the time, or that the Bow River still has no new upstream reservoir. The story answers "would it happen again?" with sourced facts and leaves viewers a two-sided question.

## 4. Research findings (labelled)
- Verified fact: June 2013 flood, largest in Calgary since 1897; heavy rainfall on melting snowpack in the Rocky Mountains (City of Calgary, Flood of 2013).
- Verified fact: Bow peaked at 2,400 m³/s, eight times its regular flow; Elbow inflow peaked at 1,240 m³/s, 12 times (City).
- Verified fact: about 80,000 people evacuated, 26 communities (City; City newsroom Jun 20, 2023: "more than 80,000").
- Verified fact: downtown inaccessible for days; at least the first ten rows of the Saddledome underwater; one Calgarian's life lost (City).
- Historical figure (assessment): flood's impact across Alberta assessed at approximately $5 billion (City; the same page also says as much as $6 billion across southern Alberta: we use the assessed figure, labelled "assessed").
- Verified fact: new 2.5 m steel gates at the Glenmore Dam doubled water storage capacity (City, Flood resilience plan).
- Verified fact: downtown flood barrier (Jaipur Bridge to Reconciliation Bridge) completed (2022 milestone, City newsroom Apr 27, 2023); Sunnyside barrier completion celebrated, about 2.4 km of protection for Sunnyside and Hillhurst (CTV News, Sept 26, 2026).
- Verified fact: Springbank Off-stream Reservoir (SR1) is a dry reservoir that stores water temporarily during a flood, about 15 km west of Calgary in Rocky View County; diverts, stores and releases Elbow River flood water; 70.2 million m³, about 28,000 Olympic pools; construction began 2022, complete 2025; built by the Government of Alberta (federal contribution $168.5M) (Government of Alberta; RenewCanada May 22, 2025; CBC News; City newsroom May 15, 2025).
- Verified fact: SR1 designed to work in tandem with the Glenmore Reservoir for volumes equal to the 2013 Elbow flood (Government of Alberta).
- City statement (attributed): Elbow River communities now protected from 2013-sized floods; flood risk reduced by about 70 per cent (City newsroom May 15, 2025; resilience plan).
- Verified fact: on the Bow, the relocated Ghost Dam option advanced in 2024 to Phase 3 Engineering & Regulatory approvals (City resilience plan, updated Nov 4, 2026).
- Interpretation: "2013 changed how Calgary lives with its rivers."
