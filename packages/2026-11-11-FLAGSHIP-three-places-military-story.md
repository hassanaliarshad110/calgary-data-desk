## 3. Why this story matters
- On Remembrance Day most Calgarians think of ceremonies and cenotaphs. The city's military history is also built into three places people pass every day: a drill hall on 11th Street SW, a southwest neighbourhood and a museum on Crowchild Trail.
- Each one has an under-told twist: Mewata Armoury, by Parks Canada's own heritage record, was completed too late to be of practical value in the First World War, then became the hub of Calgary's militia and a Second World War training camp; Currie Barracks was built as a Depression relief project and is now a neighbourhood whose historic military buildings are a Provincial Historic Resource; The Military Museums started when a surplus junior high school was turned into a museum with money Calgarians raised.
- The story connects the city's past to places viewers can visit this week.

## 5. Complete narration script (as sent to Kokoro; numbers in words)
Each numbered line is one scene.
0. Can you name three places that hold Calgary's military story?
1. This Remembrance Day, let's visit them. Start at Mewata Armoury, on Eleventh Street Southwest.
2. Built in nineteen seventeen and nineteen eighteen, it's a Tudor Gothic drill hall.
3. Here's the twist. Parks Canada's heritage record says it was finished too late to be of practical value in the First World War.
4. From nineteen eighteen to nineteen thirty-nine, it was headquarters for all Calgary militia units, and in the Second World War, the focus of a substantial training camp.
5. It's long been home to the King's Own Calgary Regiment and the Calgary Highlanders. It became a National Historic Site in nineteen eighty-nine.
6. Place two. In nineteen thirty-three, the army acquired land for a new base, named for General Sir Arthur Currie, the first Canadian to command the Canadian Corps.
7. Currie Barracks was built as a Depression relief project. And Queen Elizabeth the Second once stayed overnight in its Officers' Mess.
8. In nineteen ninety-seven, the base was decommissioned. Today it's a neighbourhood called Currie, and the old base's historic military buildings are protected as a Provincial Historic Resource.
9. Place three began as a school. When National Defence declared Sir Sam Steele Junior High surplus, Calgarians raised six and a half million dollars to make it a museum.
10. Queen Elizabeth the Second opened it as the Museum of the Regiments in nineteen ninety. In two thousand six, it became The Military Museums.
11. A drill hall finished too late for one war and busy in the next. A Depression-era base. A school turned museum. Three places, one city's way of remembering.
12. Have you visited any of these places? Which one would you show a visitor first? Drop your thoughts below.
13. Sources and the full story are at calgary insight dot c a.
