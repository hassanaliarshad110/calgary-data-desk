## 1. What happened / why it matters / what happens next
- **What happened:** On Nov 10, 2026 the City released its proposed 2027–2030 Budget. It requires a 15 per cent increase in municipal property tax revenue from existing properties in 2027.
- **Why it matters:** for a typical single residential property the City estimates about $53 more a month in 2027 ($37 municipal property tax + $16 water, waste and recycling).
- **Context, both sides:** the CAO cites growth, increased demand, rising cost and aging assets; some councillors have stressed a need to bring proposed tax hikes down.
- **What happens next:** Public Submissions begin Nov 23; Council begins deliberations the week of Nov 23; Council approval is listed for December 2026. Estimates may change through Council's deliberations.

- All dollar figures are the City's own estimates for a typical single residential property; individual bills vary with assessment and service use. No calculations of our own.

- The video does not cover the later-year tax figures, police/fire funding or capital detail (kept short and to the most-reported fact).
- No councillor or resident is named; no position is taken.
