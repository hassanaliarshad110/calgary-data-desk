# Calgary's 2027 budget: what the preliminary numbers say
Date/slot: 2026-11-09 CIVIC · Type: civic · Skill: calgary-civic-analyst · Category: Civic intelligence
Calendar topic: "Budget adjustments for 2027: what council is being asked to approve this month, by service"
**Changed angle (same category):** the 2027 budget is not an "adjustment" this year. 2027 opens a new four-year cycle (2027–2030), and the proposed budget, with its service-level detail, is only released to Council and the public on **Nov 10, 2026**, the day after this run. The only official numbers public today are the preliminary figures Council voted to release on Sept 29, 2026 (report C2026-0749, Revised Attachment 1), which give totals by year and three scenarios but **no dollar breakdown by service**. So the video explains the 2027 operating numbers, how the City's summary pairs them with council's 15% guidance, the City's scenarios, and the typical-home estimate, and says plainly that it is preliminary.

## 1. Civic issue / research question
Before the proposed 2027–2030 budget lands, what did City Administration tell council about the size of the 2027 operating gap, the tax scenarios, and what council's July guidance would mean for a typical homeowner?

## 2. Three possible angles
1. **The 2027 numbers (chosen):** $528M of new ongoing operating requests for 2027, $144M of available funding, and the remaining $384M paired on the same slide with a 15.0% total tax revenue increase for 2027, which is council's July guidance (C2026-0749, slide 28; slide 4).
2. **The scenarios:** hold the current service trend (4.2%, 4.4%, 5.0%, 4.2%) vs improve and expand (20.2%, 8.5%, 9%, 8.9%) vs deteriorate (<4.2%) (slide 10).
3. **The capital side:** $21B of 2027–2030 capital needs vs $20.5B of known financial capacity as of Sept 29, inside $54B of 2027–2035+ needs (slide 16). Kept for a later video.

## 4. Methodology and limitations
- All dollar figures are nominal by budget convention (slide 9 states "Values are nominal"; slide 28 does not say), in **$ millions**, for the **City of Calgary corporation** (tax-supported operating budget as summarised by Administration). The $528M / $144M / $384M are **2027 incremental ongoing** amounts only; they exclude one-time requests (2027: $78M requests vs $145M available) and capital. Calculation shown: 528 − 144 = 384 (matches slide 28).
- Percentages are **property tax revenue increases**, not the change on any one bill; an individual bill also depends on its assessment.
- The $36/month is the City's **estimate** for a single residential property at the **2026 median assessment of $706,000**, based on current (July) guidance, before assessments are finalized. It excludes water, waste and recycling rates (projected separately and subject to Council's October rate decisions), and excludes the provincial education property tax.
- Slide 10 does not print years next to the scenario figures; the 2027–2030 order is corroborated by Global News (Sept 2, 2026) and the Calgary Herald (Sept 30, 2026).
- Slide 28's "Total tax revenue increase required" row (15.0%, 9.8%, 7.6%, 6.0%) differs from the July guidance for 2028–2029; the deck doesn't explain the difference, so the video quotes only the 2027 figure (15%), which is the same in both.
- **No per-service dollar breakdown is public yet** in the released preliminary deck; a follow-up should look at the proposed budget released Nov 10.
- The 20.2% "improve and expand" scenario is a different, larger set than the $528M on slide 28 (Global News, from the July closed-session documents, described it as funding all requests); the video does not add them together.
- Figures shifted between versions (July closed-session figures reported by Global News: $510M, $139M; Sept 29 deck: $528M, $144M). The video uses only the Sept 29 released deck.
- The deck is a released City council attachment; the copy read is hosted by LiveWire Calgary (the eScribe agenda page could not be opened from this workspace).
- Not causal: the video does not say why requests grew; the City cites a fast-growing city and infrastructure pressures, which isn't quantified here.
- Neutral: no councillor is named; the video doesn't say whether any tax level is right.
