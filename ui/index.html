      Near, but crowded: parks and playgrounds against population in Ward 5

      Published November 5, 2026 · 2 min 9 s · narrated, figures on screen

      Almost every home in Ward 5 is within 400 m of a public park (98.6%; Calgary 98.9%), and 78.7% are within 400 m of a playground (Calgary 71.2%). But the park space within reach works out to 2.9 hectares per 1,000 residents against Calgary's 5.9, the second lowest of the 14 wards, and 2.1 playgrounds per 1,000 children under 15 against 4.1, the lowest. Homes are fuller: 3.6 people per household against 2.6 (2021 Census).

      Transcript

      Opening. Ward 5 has public parks and playgrounds close to almost every home. But how many people share them? This is amenities against population, measured the same way for every ward in Calgary.

      Distance. First, distance. Ninety-nine percent of homes in Ward 5 are within four hundred metres of a public park, about the same as Calgary as a whole. And seventy-nine percent are within four hundred metres of a playground, more than Calgary's seventy-one percent.

      Park crowding. Now, crowding. Take the park space within reach of each home, and share it among everyone who can reach it. Ward 5 has about two point nine hectares of park for every thousand residents. Calgary has five point nine. That is about half, and the second lowest of the fourteen wards.

      Playgrounds. Playgrounds tell the same story. Within four hundred metres, Ward 5 has about two playgrounds for every thousand children under fifteen, against four across Calgary. That is the lowest of any ward.

      Why. Why? Homes in Ward 5 are fuller. The 2021 Census counted three point six people per household, against two point six in Calgary, and three in ten residents were under twenty. The same parks are shared by more people, and more children.

      Community by community. Community by community, against Calgary's level of one. Redstone, Saddle Ridge, Skyview Ranch and Taradale have well under half of Calgary's park space per person. For playgrounds, Cityscape, Falconridge and Skyview Ranch are the most crowded.

      How it is measured. How it is measured: every park's area is shared among the residents within four hundred metres of its edge, and every playground among the children nearby. Each home then adds up its shares. A score of one means the same as Calgary. Every ward and community is measured the same way on the Growth versus amenities screen.

      How Calgary's population is shared across its communities

      Published November 5, 2026 · 2 min 21 s · narrated, figures on screen

      The dashboard starts from the City of Calgary's own population estimate (1,598,075 in November 2026) and places it community by community: each community's 2021 Census count, plus the people in homes built there since 2021 (from the City's property roll), plus an equal share of the rest of the City's growth, 10.0% of its 2021 count.

      Transcript

      Opening. How is Calgary's population shared across its communities? It starts with one number: the City of Calgary's own estimate, about one point six million people this November.

      The City’s figure. Step one. The City's figure comes from its Spring 2026 Economic Outlook, with an estimate for each April. Between those, we draw a straight line, month by month. Right now, that line adds about seventy people a day.

      The starting point. Step two. Each community starts from its own count in the 2021 Census: about one point two nine million people in all. Since then, the City's figure has added about three hundred and nine thousand.

      Placing the growth. Step three. Where do those people go? About one hundred and eighty thousand live in homes built since 2021, so they are placed in the community where those homes were built. The other one hundred and twenty nine thousand come from more people in each home, seniors' homes, residences, shelters, and people the Census missed. That part is shared by every community: ten percent on top of its 2021 count.

      How new homes are counted. New homes come from the City's property roll. We keep residential accounts with a home on them, and leave out vacant lots, commercial property, parking stalls and storage lockers. A new home counts as lived in one year after it is built, and a rental building counts by its units.

      The same rule for every community. Step four. Every community follows the same rule: its 2021 count, times one point one, plus the people in its new homes. Abbeydale, with no new homes, grows to six thousand five hundred and twenty. Cornerstone, with about thirty two hundred new homes, reaches eighteen thousand one hundred and eighty five. Together, every community adds up to the City's figure.

      Where the new homes are. This is where the new homes are. The most people in new homes are on the edges of the city: Cornerstone, Livingston, Mahogany, Carrington and Seton.

        Your reports

        Made here or in Data Genie, newest first. Every report is also in Files.

        Report auditor

        Three separate checks on a finished report. Chinook (the auditor) re-runs every figure from the data and lists what is wrong. Bow (the reviewer) re-checks each finding itself, accepts, revises or rejects it with its reasons, and makes the corrected report. Rundle (the final check) then looks only at those findings: fixed correctly, or rightly rejected. About 5 to 15 minutes a report.

        1. Chinook: re-runs every figure
