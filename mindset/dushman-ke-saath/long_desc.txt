You have power over an enemy. Right now. What do you do?

Two men faced that moment, centuries apart. A duke in Renaissance Italy trusted an enemy he had wronged, and it ruined him. A warrior in one of Rumi's stories was insulted by the man he had beaten, and what he did next won something no battle could give him. Machiavelli and Rumi, mind vs heart, on one of the oldest questions there is.

Quotes used (public-domain translations):
"He who believes that new benefits will cause great personages to forget old injuries is deceived."
"The duke erred in his choice, and it was the cause of his ultimate ruin."
"For men injure either from fear or hatred."
— The Prince, Ch. 7 (tr. W. K. Marriott, 1908)
"...by them to mount higher, as by a ladder which his enemies have raised."
— The Prince, Ch. 20 (tr. W. K. Marriott, 1908)
"'Ali, instead of taking vengeance on him, at once dropped his sword, to the Magian's great astonishment."
— Masnavi, Book I, Story XVI "'Ali's Forbearance" (E. H. Whinfield's prose summary of Rumi's story)

Try this today: think of one rival. On the left of a page, write what they could really do to you (be honest, not hopeful). On the right, write what your anger wants to do to them, and then don't do it. Pick one move that makes you stronger instead.

Which is harder for you: seeing an enemy clearly, or putting down the sword? Mind, or heart? Tell me in the comments 👇
